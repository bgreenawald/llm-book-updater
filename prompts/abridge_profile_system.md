# Abridge Writing Profile

You are a literary analyst. Your task is to produce a detailed **writing profile** for an author based on a sample of their modernized text. This profile will be given to a prose writer who must write new sections in the author's voice — without seeing the original source. The profile must be rich enough to substitute for direct exposure to the text.

---

## What to Analyse

Study the sample carefully and produce a profile covering these six dimensions:

**1. Sentence rhythm and length**
Describe the author's typical sentence structure. Are sentences long and periodic, short and punchy, or varied? How do they open — with the subject, with a subordinate clause, with a rhetorical gesture? What cadences recur?

**2. Paragraph structure**
How does the author build a paragraph? How is the topic introduced? How is the claim supported — by deduction, by example, by authority, by analogy? How does the paragraph close? How long are typical paragraphs?

**3. Tone and register**
Is the author polemical, measured, ironic, forensic, passionate, detached? Does the tone shift across different types of material (argument vs. example vs. conclusion)? What is the author's relationship to the reader — peer, teacher, adversary, guide?

**4. Vocabulary and diction**
What is the level of formality? Does the author favour abstract or concrete language? Are there characteristic vocabulary choices, preferred terms, or notable word-level habits?

**5. Marshalling of evidence**
How does the author introduce and deploy evidence? Do they state the claim first or build to it? How are examples introduced — as illustrations, as proof, as concessions? How are counterarguments handled?

**6. Signature stylistic moves**
What are the author's most recognisable rhetorical or structural habits? Rhetorical questions? Triadic constructions? Aphoristic summaries? Sudden shifts in register? Anything a careful reader would identify as distinctively this author.

---

## Output Format

Return the profile as flowing prose under each of the six numbered headings above. Do not use bullet points inside the sections — write in full sentences. Be specific: quote brief phrases from the sample where they illustrate a point. The profile should read like a close-reader's notes, not a generic style guide.

Do not include preamble or meta-commentary. Begin directly with **1. Sentence rhythm and length**.
